Privacy

Last updated July 2026

At Spaceland Studios, we value our players' privacy. Short version: we use accounts and cookies strictly to run the game and let you pick up where you left off — not to build a profile on you, sell your data, or track you around the web. Here's the honest, specific breakdown of what actually happens when you play.

If you sign in

Signing in with Google, Discord, or GitHub is optional. If you do, we get your name, your avatar photo from that provider, and an account ID — that's it. We never see your password; the login itself happens entirely on Google's/Discord's/GitHub's side, not ours. This is what lets the game recognize you're the same person if you refresh or come back later, and stops one person from holding two seats in the same room.

You don't need an account to play. Pick a name, you're in.

While you're actually playing

Your display name, the color you picked, the sentences you write, and your score are visible to everyone else in your room while the game's running. That's the whole point of a party game — it's not being logged or sold anywhere, it just lives in the server's memory for as long as your game does and disappears when the room empties out.

What's stored on your device

A few small things live in your browser's local storage — your name, language, and sound preferences — so you don't have to re-enter them every time. There's also a session cookie that tells the server "this browser is this player," which is how a page refresh doesn't kick you out of a game mid-round. None of this gets sent anywhere except back to our own server.

Ads

This site runs ads through Google AdSense, which means Google may set its own cookies to help decide what ads to show you, based on sites you've visited before. That's Google's system, not ours — if you'd rather not have personalized ads, you can turn that off directly at google.com/settings/ads, and their full explanation of how this works is at policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

The AI judge

If a match has AI-judged voting turned on, the finished story and each player's secret goal get sent to Google's Gemini API so it can rule on who actually pulled off their goal. Nobody's name or account info goes along with that request — just the story text.

Kids

This game isn't aimed at children under 13, and we're not intentionally collecting anything from them.
